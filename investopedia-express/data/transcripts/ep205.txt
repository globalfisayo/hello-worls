Inflation.
Inflation measures the rise in prices of goods and services over time, and it has also been
the dominant economic theme in the US since the beginning of the recovery from the COVID
pandemic in 2021.
A combination of trillions of dollars in stimulus funds and other government spending, combined
with a shortage of goods, spiked the inflation rate to a multi-decade high of 8% in 2022,
forcing the Federal Reserve to sharply raise interest rates to bring it closer to its
target of 2%.
Meme stocks.
Every generation has its stock market mania and for the COVID era it was meme stocks.
A new generation of day traders embraced these highly speculative and highly shorted stocks
like GameStop and AMC Entertainment.
Stuck at home with stimulus checks, day traders were using social media community platforms
such as Reddit's Wall Street Bets to encourage one another to bid these stocks higher and
then hold on with Diamond Hands to rake in some tendies.
Meme stocks gave birth to this new trading vocabulary and popularized market sensations
like Roaring Kitty and Dave Portnoy. And, like all stock market manias, this one didn't
end well either. Many mean stocks lost up to 90% of their value between 2021 and 2024.
The inverted yield curve. When the yield on long-term US Treasury debt interest rates
is less than short-term interest rates, it's a signal that investors are pretty pessimistic
about the near-term future of the economy and it's a pretty reliable harbinger of a
recession. At least an inverted yield curve used to be a sign that a downturn was on
its way. However, beginning in July of 2022, the yield curve in the US Treasury bonds of
various durations was inverted for over 650 days, the longest stretch on record, and there was no
recession. While an inverted yield curve may not be as reliable a signal of an economic downturn
as it used to be, investors are still fascinated by this location in the US Treasury market.
Artificial Intelligence

John McCarthy. AI, as it's commonly referred to, exploded into the investing universe in 2022
as the biggest companies in the world, such as Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, and NVIDIA,
devoted tens of billions of dollars of R&D money to using machine learning across their products
and services. Artificial intelligence has become a buzzword for all kinds of supercomputing needs
like data processing, machine learning, learn language models, and consumer-facing applications

the market-weighted S&P 500 and the NASDAQ to record highs as the biggest companies in those
indexes are worth trillions of dollars as investors bet on the promise of this developing technology.
