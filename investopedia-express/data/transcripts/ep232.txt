It's terminology time. Time for us to get smart with the investing in finance term we need to
know this week. And this week's term is brought to us by the credit markets. Fixed income, bonds,
that is. And this week's term is credit spreads. No, not soft cheese you spread over a Triskit,
but according to the world's greatest investing education website, it's the difference in yield
between corporate bonds and risk-free government bonds of the same maturity. The wider the credit
spread, the higher the risk and the more likelihood of trouble brewing in the market.
The theory being that if corporate debt yields are spiking because investors are demanding
more risk to hold their bonds, something may be rotten under their balance sheets.
Up until a couple of weeks ago, big institutional investors weren't that worried about the
health of the capital markets because credit spreads were pretty narrow.
They hadn't blown out or widened too much despite the sell-off in stocks.
Sorry to say, that's not the case anymore.
At least with junk bonds or riskier corporate bonds.
ETFs that track and hold junk bonds have been tumbling in price over the past week
as yields on their bond holdings are spiking, and that has the drum beat about a possible
recession getting louder and louder. Widening spreads lead to tighter credit conditions,
as higher borrowing costs make it harder for businesses to raise capital for expansion,
hiring and operation. This slows down economic growth and can lead to layoffs, which causes
investors to seek safety by shifting money from corporate bonds into safer government
bonds, pushing Treasury yields lower. This risk-off behavior often precedes economic
downturns and, sorry to say, recessions. We saw it in 2020 for a few minutes during the
COVID lockdown, but we really saw it in 2008 in the fires of the great financial crisis.
The economy today is, or was until five weeks ago in much better shape than it was in 2008
and 2020. But uncertainty around tariffs, layoffs, and just about everything Washington
touches these days could turn things upside down and cause those spreads to widen out.
