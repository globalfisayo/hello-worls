It's terminology time. Time for us to get smart with the investing term we need to know this
week. And never underestimate Wall Street's ability to come up with a new strategy or dust
off an old one to tempt us into buying it to chase better returns. It's how this street
Treat has always worked, and the latest reincarnation of it is the Portable Alpha Strategy, our
term and strategy of the week.
According to the world's greatest investing education website, the Portable Alpha Strategy
focuses on investing in stocks or other assets that have demonstrated little or no correlation
with the markets.
To do this, investors separate Alpha from Beta by investing in securities that are
not in the market index from which their Beta is derived.
In other words, pick stocks or derivatives that are not part of the herd, and lately
big investors have been piling into this particular flavor of portable alpha that uses derivatives
to track returns of long-only indexes and then invest the excess cash in trades like
trend following or market neutral equity strategies.
Barclays reports that 22% of institutional investors, private banks and family offices
adopted some form of portable alpha as a form of asset allocation last year.
That's up from 10% the previous year.
And of course, there are new ETFs out there that want to capture this strategy, which
which is also referred to as return stacking.
Can't you just see yachts with that name or fleece Patagonia vests with that logo on
it?
The problem is that Portable Alpha was also one of the strategies that failed miserably
during the global financial crisis.
When all markets head south, the diversification value collapses and there is no alpha.
When this happens, traders and investors trapped in Portable Alpha strategies can't
exit illiquid positions and that's when the bottom falls out.
Beware of new strategies in tricky markets, especially ones that are coming back into
style just as the markets begin to wobble.
